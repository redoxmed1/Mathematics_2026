### Exercise 10. A Polynomial Through Three Points

**Problem**

Find a polynomial

$$
p(x)=ax^2+bx+c
$$

whose graph passes through $(0,1)$, $(1,3)$, and $(2,9)$.

Reduce the problem to a system of equations and check the result using all three points.

**Solution**

The polynomial is

$$
p(x)=ax^2+bx+c
$$

Substitute each point.

For the point $(0,1)$:

$$
c=1
$$

For the point $(1,3)$:

$$
a+b+c=3
$$

For the point $(2,9)$:

$$
4a+2b+c=9
$$

The resulting system is

$$
\begin{cases}
c=1, \\
a+b+c=3, \\
4a+2b+c=9.
\end{cases}
$$

From the first equation,

$$
c=1
$$

The second equation gives

$$
a+b=2
$$

The third equation gives

$$
4a+2b=8
$$

or

$$
2a+b=4
$$

Subtract the equations:

$$
(2a+b)-(a+b)=4-2
$$

Therefore,

$$
a=2
$$

Then

$$
b=0
$$

and

$$
c=1
$$

The polynomial is

$$
p(x)=2x^2+1
$$

**Check**

At $x=0$:

$$
p(0)=2(0)^2+1=1
$$

At $x=1$:

$$
p(1)=2(1)^2+1=3
$$

At $x=2$:

$$
p(2)=2(2)^2+1=9
$$

All three points satisfy the polynomial.
