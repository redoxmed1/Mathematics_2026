### Exercise 2. A Line in Space

**Problem**

Given

$$
L:(x,y,z)=(2,-1,3)+t(1,2,-2).
$$

State a point on the line and its direction vector, check whether $P=(4,3,-1)$ lies on $L$, and find the point corresponding to $t=-1$.

**Solution**

**1. Identifying a point and direction vector**

The parametric equation of a line has the form:

$$
L:p+tv
$$

Comparing with the given equation:

$$
p=(2,-1,3)
$$

$$
v=(1,2,-2)
$$

Therefore, a point on the line is $(2,-1,3)$ and its direction vector is $(1,2,-2)$.

**2. Checking whether P lies on the line**

The given point is:

$$
P=(4,3,-1)
$$

To check whether it belongs to the line, we compare its coordinates with the parametric equations.

$$
\begin{cases}
x=2+t, \\
y=-1+2t, \\
z=3-2t.
\end{cases}
$$

Using the first coordinate:

$$
4=2+t
$$

$$
t=2
$$

Check the second coordinate:

$$
y=-1+2(2)=3
$$

Check the third coordinate:

$$
z=3-2(2)=-1
$$

All three coordinates match.

Therefore, the point belongs to the line.

**3. Finding the point for t = -1**

Substitute $t=-1$ into the parametric equation:

$$
(x,y,z)=(2,-1,3)+(-1)(1,2,-2)
$$

$$
=(2,-1,3)-(1,2,-2)
$$

$$
=(1,-3,5)
$$

**Final Results**

Point on the line:

$$
p=(2,-1,3)
$$

Direction vector:

$$
v=(1,2,-2)
$$

The point $P=(4,3,-1)$ belongs to the line.

For $t=-1$:

$$
(x,y,z)=(1,-3,5)
$$
