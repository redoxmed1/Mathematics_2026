### Exercise 3. Plane Through a Point with a Given Normal Vector

**Problem**

Find the equation of the plane through $P=(1,-2,3)$ and perpendicular to $n=(2,1,-1)$. Check the result by substituting the point $P$ and explain the role of the normal vector.

**Solution**

**1. Understanding the normal vector**

A normal vector is perpendicular to every direction lying in the plane.

The general equation of a plane is:

$$
ax+by+cz+d=0
$$

The coefficients $a,b,c$ are the coordinates of the normal vector.

Given:

$$
n=(2,1,-1)
$$

Therefore, the plane has the form:

$$
2x+y-z+d=0
$$

**2. Finding the constant d**

The plane passes through:

$$
P=(1,-2,3)
$$

Substitute the coordinates into the equation:

$$
2(1)+(-2)-3+d=0
$$

$$
2-2-3+d=0
$$

$$
-3+d=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
d=3
$$

**3. Writing the plane equation**

Substituting the value of $d$:

$$
2x+y-z+3=0
$$

**4. Checking the point**

Substitute $P=(1,-2,3)$:

$$
2(1)+(-2)-3+3=0
$$

$$
2-2-3+3=0
$$

$$
0=0
$$

The point satisfies the plane equation.

**5. Geometric interpretation**

The normal vector $(2,1,-1)$ determines the orientation of the plane.

It is perpendicular to every vector contained in the plane.

The point $P$ determines the position of the plane in space.

**Final Result**

$$
2x+y-z+3=0
$$

Normal vector:

$$
n=(2,1,-1)
$$
