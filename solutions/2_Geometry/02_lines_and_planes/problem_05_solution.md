### Exercise 5. Relative Position of Two Lines

**Problem**

Analyze the position of

$$
L_1:(x,y,z)=(1,1,0)+t(1,2,1),
$$

$$
L_2:(x,y,z)=(2,0,1)+s(0,1,-1).
$$

If they intersect, give the intersection point. If not, determine whether they are parallel or skew. Justify the classification.

**Solution**

**1. Identifying the direction vectors**

For the first line:

$$
p_1=(1,1,0)
$$

$$
v_1=(1,2,1)
$$

For the second line:

$$
p_2=(2,0,1)
$$

$$
v_2=(0,1,-1)
$$

**2. Checking whether the lines are parallel**

Two lines are parallel if their direction vectors are scalar multiples of each other.

The vectors are:

$$
v_1=(1,2,1)
$$

$$
v_2=(0,1,-1)
$$

Their first coordinates are different in a way that prevents one vector from being a scalar multiple of the other.

Therefore, the lines are not parallel.

**3. Checking whether the lines intersect**

Write the coordinate equations:

For $L_1$:

$$
\begin{cases}
x=1+t, \\
y=1+2t, \\
z=t.
\end{cases}
$$

For $L_2$:

$$
\begin{cases}
x=2, \\
y=s, \\
z=1-s.
\end{cases}
$$

If the lines intersect, their corresponding coordinates must be equal.

From the first coordinates:

$$
1+t=2
$$

$$
t=1
$$

From the second coordinates:

$$
1+2(1)=s
$$

$$
s=3
$$

Now check the third coordinates:

$$
t=1
$$

$$
1-s=1-3=-2
$$

Since:

$$
1\neq -2
$$

The three coordinate equations cannot be satisfied simultaneously.

Therefore, the lines do not intersect.

**4. Classifying the lines**

We have established that:

- The direction vectors are not scalar multiples, so the lines are not parallel.
- The coordinate equations have no common solution, so the lines do not intersect.

Two nonparallel lines in three-dimensional space that do not intersect are called skew lines.

**Final Result**

The lines are skew.

They are neither parallel nor intersecting.
