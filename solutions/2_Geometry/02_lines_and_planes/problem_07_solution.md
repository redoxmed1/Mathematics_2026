### Exercise 7. A Line Relative to a Plane

**Problem**

For

$$
L:(x,y,z)=(1,1,0)+t(1,-1,0),
$$

$$
\Pi:x+y+z=2,
$$

determine whether the line intersects the plane at one point, is parallel to it, or lies in it. Explain the role of the dot product of the direction vector and the normal vector.

**Solution**

**1. Identifying the direction vector**

From the line equation:

$$
p=(1,1,0)
$$

$$
v=(1,-1,0)
$$

The direction vector is:

$$
v=(1,-1,0)
$$

**2. Identifying the normal vector**

The plane is:

$$
x+y+z=2
$$

Its normal vector is:

$$
n=(1,1,1)
$$

**3. Computing the dot product**

Calculate:

$$
v\cdot n=1(1)+(-1)(1)+0(1)
$$

$$
v\cdot n=1-1+0
$$

$$
v\cdot n=0
$$

Since the dot product is zero, the direction vector is perpendicular to the normal vector.

This means the line is parallel to the plane or contained in it.

**4. Checking whether the line lies in the plane**

The starting point of the line is:

$$
p=(1,1,0)
$$

Substitute it into the plane equation:

$$
1+1+0=2
$$

The point belongs to the plane.

Since the direction vector is parallel to the plane and the starting point belongs to the plane, the entire line lies in the plane.

**5. Geometric interpretation**

The dot product determines the relationship between the direction of the line and the normal vector of the plane.

When the dot product is zero, the line is parallel to the plane or contained in it.

Checking the starting point distinguishes these two cases.

**Final Result**

$$
v\cdot n=0
$$

The starting point belongs to the plane.

Therefore, the entire line lies in the plane.
