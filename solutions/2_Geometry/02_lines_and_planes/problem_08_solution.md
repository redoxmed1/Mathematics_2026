### Exercise 8. Intersection Point of a Line and a Plane

**Problem**

Find the intersection point of

$$
(x,y,z)=(1,0,2)+t(1,2,-1)
$$

with the plane $x+y+z=4$. Check the result by substituting it into both descriptions.

**Solution**

**1. Writing the parametric equations**

The line is:

$$
(x,y,z)=(1,0,2)+t(1,2,-1)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\begin{cases}
x=1+t, \\
y=2t, \\
z=2-t.
\end{cases}
$$

**2. Substituting into the plane equation**

The plane is:

$$
x+y+z=4
$$

Substitute the parametric coordinates:

$$
(1+t)+2t+(2-t)=4
$$

Simplifying:

$$
3+2t=4
$$

$$
2t=1
$$

$$
t=\frac12
$$

**3. Finding the intersection point**

Substitute $t=\frac12$ into the line:

$$
x=1+\frac12=\frac32
$$

$$
y=2\left(\frac12\right)=1
$$

$$
z=2-\frac12=\frac32
$$

Therefore, the intersection point is:

$$
I=\left(\frac32,1,\frac32\right)
$$

**4. Checking the result in the line equation**

Using $t=\frac12$:

$$
(x,y,z)=
(1,0,2)+\frac12(1,2,-1)
$$

$$
=\left(\frac32,1,\frac32\right)
$$

The point satisfies the line equation.

**5. Checking the result in the plane equation**

Substitute the intersection point:

$$
\frac32+1+\frac32=4
$$

$$
4=4
$$

The point also satisfies the plane equation.

**Final Result**

The intersection point is:

$$
I=\left(\frac32,1,\frac32\right)
$$
