### Exercise 5. Hyperbola and Asymptotes

**Problem**

For

x²/16 - y²/9 = 1

find the intercepts with the x-axis, the asymptotes, and make a sketch. Explain why the asymptotes describe the behavior of the curve far from the center.

**Solution**

**Step 1. Identify the canonical form**

The canonical equation of a horizontal hyperbola is:

x²/a² - y²/b² = 1

Comparing:

a² = 16

b² = 9

Therefore:

a = 4

b = 3

**Step 2. Find the center**

The center is:

C = (0, 0)

**Step 3. Find the x-intercepts**

Set:

y = 0

Substitute:

x²/16 = 1

x² = 16

x = ±4

Therefore, the intercepts are:

(4, 0) and (-4, 0)

**Step 4. Find the asymptotes**

For a horizontal hyperbola, the asymptotes are:

y = ±(b/a)x

Substitute a = 4 and b = 3:

y = (3/4)x

y = -(3/4)x

**Step 5. Explain the asymptotes**

The hyperbola has two branches opening to the left and right.

Far from the center, the branches get closer and closer to the asymptote lines.

The asymptotes describe the direction in which the branches extend.

The branches approach these lines but never touch them.

**Final Answer**

- Center: C = (0, 0)
- a = 4
- b = 3
- x-intercepts: (4, 0), (-4, 0)
- Asymptotes: y = (3/4)x and y = -(3/4)x
- The hyperbola opens to the left and right.
