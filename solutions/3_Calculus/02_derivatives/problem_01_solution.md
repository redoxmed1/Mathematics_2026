# Exercise 1: Derivative of a Polynomial and the Slope of a Tangent Line

## Problem

For

$$
f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4
$$

find $f'(x)$. Then compute $f'(0)$ and $f'(1)$, determine the signs of the obtained values, and explain what they say about the slope of the tangent line to the graph at these two points.

## Solution

### Step 1: Calculate the derivative

We use the power rule:

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(x^n)=nx^{n-1}
$$

We differentiate each term separately:

$$
f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4
$$

Therefore,

$$
f'(x)=3(5x^4)-2(3x^2)+7(1)-0
$$

Simplifying each term:

$$
f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7
$$

Thus,

$$
\boxed{f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7}
$$

### Step 2: Calculate $f'(0)$

Substitute $x=0$ into the derivative:

$$
f'(0)=15(0)^4-6(0)^2+7
$$

$$
f'(0)=0-0+7
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{f'(0)=7}
$$

The result is positive, so the tangent line has a positive slope at $x=0$.

### Step 3: Calculate $f'(1)$

Substitute $x=1$:

$$
f'(1)=15(1)^4-6(1)^2+7
$$

$$
f'(1)=15-6+7
$$

$$
\boxed{f'(1)=16}
$$

This result is also positive, so the tangent line has a positive slope at $x=1$.

### Step 4: Interpret the results

The derivative represents the slope of the tangent line to the graph.

- At $x=0$, the slope is $7$, so the graph is increasing at that point.
- At $x=1$, the slope is $16$, so the graph is also increasing at that point.

Both slopes are positive, but the tangent line at $x=1$ is steeper because its slope is greater.

## Final Answer

$$
\boxed{f'(x)=15x^4-6x^2+7}
$$

$$
\boxed{f'(0)=7>0}
$$

$$
\boxed{f'(1)=16>0}
$$
